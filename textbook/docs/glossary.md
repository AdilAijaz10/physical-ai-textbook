# Glossary

## A

**Action (in robotics)**: A specific behavior or movement that a robot can perform, such as moving forward, grasping an object, or turning.

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)**: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems, including learning, reasoning, and self-correction.

**Autonomous Robot**: A robot that can operate independently without human intervention, making decisions based on sensor input and pre-programmed or learned behaviors.

## B

**Balance Control**: The ability of a humanoid robot to maintain its center of mass within its support polygon to avoid falling.

**Bipedal Locomotion**: The act of walking on two legs, which is the primary mode of movement for humanoid robots.

**Behavior Tree**: A hierarchical structure used in robotics and game AI to organize and control the execution of tasks and behaviors.

## C

**Computer Vision**: A field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world using digital images, videos, and deep learning models.

**Control System**: A system that manages, commands, directs, or regulates the behavior of other devices or systems in robotics.

**Cognitive Planning**: The process of using high-level reasoning to create sequences of actions that achieve specific goals, often using large language models.

## D

**Deep Learning**: A subset of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks with multiple layers to model and understand complex patterns.

**Digital Twin**: A virtual replica of a physical system that can be used for simulation, testing, and optimization purposes.

**Domain Randomization**: A technique in robotics simulation that involves randomizing various aspects of the simulation environment to improve the transfer of learned behaviors to the real world.

## E

**Embodied AI**: Artificial intelligence that is integrated with a physical system (like a robot) and can interact with the real world.

**End Effector**: The device at the end of a robotic arm that interacts with the environment, such as a gripper or tool.

**Environment Mapping**: The process of creating a representation of the robot's surroundings for navigation and planning purposes.

## F

**Forward Kinematics**: The process of determining the position and orientation of the robot's end effector based on the joint angles.

**Fiducial Marker**: A visual marker with a known pattern that can be detected by computer vision systems for localization and tracking.

## G

**Gazebo**: A 3D dynamic simulator used in robotics for simulating robots in complex environments with realistic physics.

**Gait**: The pattern of movement of the limbs in locomotion, especially in bipedal walking.

**Generalization**: The ability of a trained model to perform well on new, unseen data or environments.

## H

**Humanoid Robot**: A robot with a physical form that approximates the human body, typically having a head, torso, two arms, and two legs.

**Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)**: The study of interactions between humans and robots, including communication, collaboration, and social aspects.

## I

**Inverse Kinematics**: The process of determining the joint angles required to position the robot's end effector at a desired position and orientation.

**Isaac ROS**: NVIDIA's collection of GPU-accelerated perception and navigation packages built for ROS 2.

**Isaac Sim**: NVIDIA's next-generation robotics simulation application built on NVIDIA Omniverse.

## J

**Joint Space**: The space defined by the robot's joint angles, as opposed to Cartesian space which is defined by position and orientation.

## K

**Kinematics**: The study of motion without considering the forces that cause the motion, particularly relevant for robot arm and leg movement.

## L

**Large Language Model (LLM)**: Advanced AI models trained on vast amounts of text data that can understand and generate human-like language.

**LiDAR**: Light Detection and Ranging, a remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure distances.

**Localization**: The process of determining the robot's position and orientation within a known or unknown environment.

**Loop Closure**: In SLAM, the recognition of previously visited locations to correct accumulated errors in the map.

## M

**Machine Learning**: A type of artificial intelligence that allows software applications to become more accurate at predicting outcomes without being explicitly programmed to do so.

**Manipulation**: The ability of a robot to purposefully change the state of objects in its environment, typically using robotic arms and grippers.

**Mapping**: The process of creating a representation of the environment for navigation and planning purposes.

**Multimodal**: Systems that can process and integrate information from multiple sensory modalities (e.g., vision, audio, touch).

## N

**Navigation**: The ability of a robot to move through its environment from one location to another, typically involving path planning and obstacle avoidance.

**Neural Network**: A computing system inspired by the human brain, consisting of interconnected nodes that process information in a distributed manner.

**Node (in ROS)**: A process that performs computation in the ROS system, representing a single executable that uses ROS communication functionality.

## O

**Obstacle Avoidance**: The ability of a robot to detect and navigate around obstacles in its environment.

**Odometry**: The use of data from motion sensors to estimate change in position over time, commonly used for robot localization.

**Omniverse**: NVIDIA's simulation and collaboration platform for 3D design workflows, used as the foundation for Isaac Sim.

## P

**Path Planning**: The computational process of determining a collision-free path from a start to a goal position in an environment.

**Perception**: The ability of a robot to interpret sensory information from its environment, including vision, touch, and other sensors.

**Physically-Based Rendering (PBR)**: A method of shading and rendering that more accurately simulates how light interacts with surfaces for realistic appearance.

**Physics Engine**: Software that simulates physical systems, calculating forces, velocities, and collisions in robotic simulations.

**Point Cloud**: A set of data points in space, typically representing the external surface of an object, often generated by 3D scanners or LiDAR.

## R

**Real-time**: Systems that process and respond to input within a guaranteed time frame, essential for robot control and safety.

**Reinforcement Learning**: A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by performing actions and receiving rewards or penalties.

**Robot Operating System (ROS)**: Flexible framework for writing robot software, providing services designed for a heterogeneous computer cluster.

**ROS 2**: The second generation of the Robot Operating System with improved features for real-world deployment.

**Rviz**: The 3D visualization tool for ROS that allows users to view robot models, sensor data, and other information.

## S

**Sensor Fusion**: The process of combining sensory data from multiple sources to achieve better accuracy and reliability than could be achieved by using a single sensor.

**Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)**: The computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously keeping track of an agent's location within it.

**State Estimation**: The process of determining the current state of a robot, including position, velocity, and other relevant parameters.

**Stereo Vision**: A technique that extracts 3D information from two or more 2D images taken from different viewpoints.

## T

**Task Planning**: The process of creating a sequence of actions to achieve a specific goal, considering the robot's capabilities and environmental constraints.

**Trajectory Planning**: The process of determining the path and timing of motion for a robot's joints or end effector.

## U

**URDF (Unified Robot Description Format)**: An XML format for representing a robot model including kinematic and dynamic properties.

**Unity**: A cross-platform game engine that can be used for creating realistic robotics simulations and visualizations.

**Unstructured Environment**: An environment with unpredictable elements, obstacles, and configurations, as opposed to a controlled laboratory setting.

## V

**VSLAM (Visual SLAM)**: Simultaneous Localization and Mapping using visual sensors such as cameras.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA)**: Systems that integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and physical action capabilities.

**Virtual Reality (VR)**: A simulated experience that can be similar to or completely different from the real world, used for training and testing robots.

## W

**Waypoint**: A reference point on a path or route used for navigation, representing a specific location that a robot should reach.

**Whisper**: OpenAI's automatic speech recognition system that can transcribe speech to text with high accuracy across multiple languages.

## X

**Xacro**: An XML macro language for the Robot Operating System that allows for more efficient and readable URDF files through the use of macros and mathematical expressions.

## Y

**Yaw**: The rotation of a robot around its vertical axis, changing its heading direction.

## Z

**Zero Moment Point (ZMP)**: A concept used in robotics and biomechanics to propose a stability criterion for bipedal robots during walking or standing.
